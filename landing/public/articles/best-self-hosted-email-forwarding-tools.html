Best Self-Hosted Email Forwarding Tools Compared (Open Source, MIT)

A practical comparison of the best self-hosted email forwarding tools: architecture, cost, and which open source email forwarding tool fits a Cloudflare-based setup.

If you want an open source email forwarding tool you run yourself, the options fall into two camps: full mail servers (Postfix-based) and lightweight forwarders built on Cloudflare. For most people, the second camp wins.

What to look for in a self-hosted forwarder

  • Where your mail lives — a Worker on your own Cloudflare account keeps data in your account; a VPS means you operate the box.
  • Operational cost — Cloudflare’s free tier covers 100k requests/day; a VPS is a monthly bill plus patching.
  • Management surface — can you edit rules, search mail, and see stats without SSH-ing in?

Why a Cloudflare-based tool wins for most

A self-hosted email forwarding tool on Cloudflare gives you the best of both worlds: self-hosted (your account, your data) and managed (a panel instead of config files). You keep one catch-all rule in Email Routing; the tool handles matching, filtering, storing and counting — with an MIT license and zero server maintenance.

InboxFly is built exactly this way: open source email forwarding with a real management panel, deployable to your own Cloudflare account in minutes.

FAQ

Is a Cloudflare-based forwarder reliable? Yes — Cloudflare Email Routing is production infrastructure, and Workers process mail synchronously with fail-open fallbacks.

Can I migrate from ForwardEmail or a Postfix setup? Usually yes: point the MX records to Cloudflare Email Routing, import your rules, and update your destination addresses.
